Klaviyo-driven revenue was $863,092 this summer, down from $945,252 last summer — a real but explainable 8.7% decline. Engagement itself held up: open rates, click rates and deliverability are flat to better than last year, and flow conversion rates actually improved. What changed is volume and targeting on campaigns, and order value inside cart-recovery flows. June was even slightly ahead of last June; the gap is concentrated in July and August.

Email campaigns
$283,545−9.5% YoY

Sent about 30% fewer campaigns (77 → 54) to a broader list — median audience per send is up 75%, from 116.5K to 204.0K. Last summer paired big sends with small, high-converting drops like Liverpool Bucks reminders; most of that targeted layer is gone. Revenue per recipient fell 38.6%. Two blockbuster Labor Day sends alone carried $45.4K last August; nothing this year came close.

SMS campaigns
$47,348−24.0% YoY

Same pattern, sharper: more sends (18→22) to a bigger list (+65% recipients), but revenue per recipient down 54%. Worth noting: 2025 had zero SMS sends in August (program went quiet after July 25). Comparing the overlapping June–July window only, SMS is down 45.6%, not 24% — the headline number is actually flattered by August 2026 having SMS at all.

Flows
$532,199−6.6% YoY

The most stable channel. Flow deliveries are up 61% and orders are up 6.8% — more people are flowing through automations and buying. But revenue per delivery fell 42%. Cart-recovery flows (Added to Cart + Abandoned Checkout) are the single biggest driver, down $62.5K (−24.3%) on lower order value, not lower engagement. Loyalty & rewards flows are up 54.6% and are a genuine bright spot.

Worth flagging
Cart recovery lost value per order, not orders. Added to Cart delivered almost the same volume as last year (−1%) with a slightly better click rate, but AOV inside the flow fell from $194 to $162 (−16%). Abandoned Checkout saw real volume loss too (−24% delivered, fewer people abandoning at checkout in the first place). Together: −$62.5K, the largest single driver in the audit.
Campaigns shifted from targeted drops to broad blasts. About 30% fewer sends, median audience per send up 75%. The Liverpool Bucks loyalty-reminder cadence that drove $17.5K last summer is down to $3.0K (−82.5%) this year on far fewer sends.
Welcome Series had a slow June, already recovered. The SMS welcome flow was rebuilt this year: $5K in June 2026 vs. $32.5K in June 2025, but $23K in July and $41K in August — back on pace. Combined welcome revenue is down 15.2% for the summer, concentrated almost entirely in that June transition.
Not everything is down. Loyalty & rewards flows (+54.6%), Back in Stock and related flows (+173.6%), and win-back/review flows (+11.3%) all grew. Deliverability also improved — email bounce rate, spam complaints, and unsubscribes are all lower than last summer.
