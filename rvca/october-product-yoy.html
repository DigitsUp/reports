The read

Sales are up overall. The misses sit in summer basics and the core volume lines.

  1. The total is not the problem. Net sales grew 14% on 12% fewer units, carried by fall product: jackets, jeans and chinos, flannels and thermals. Discounting was lighter (discounts were 8.5% of gross vs 11.1%).
  2. The misses are concentrated in the high-volume, lower-ticket lines: tees, walkshorts, tanks, hoodies and hats. Together those five types lost about $25.6K year over year.
  3. The single biggest miss is the Yogger short lineup, down $9.4K. The core Yogger Stretch 17" held flat. The drop is the variants that sold last October and have no sales this October (2-in-1, Hawaii, Lite 15", Hybrid, collab and artist versions), which added up to about $12.2K last year.
  4. Most product-level “misses” are line changes, not lost demand. 63% of this period’s sales came from products with no sales last October, and products that sold in both periods are up 18%. Reading the gaps at the franchise level is more reliable than product by product.
  5. Returns are the drag to watch. They rose 82% to $33,971 and absorbed about a third of the gross sales increase.

03

The big misses, line by line

Franchise-level reads, so renamed and replacement styles are counted together. Dollar figures are net sales change vs last October.

Yogger shorts

−$9.4K

$23.3K vs $32.7K, units 409 vs 647. Yogger Stretch 17" held at $17.6K (vs $17.9K). The gap is the variant lineup: last October’s 2-in-1 ($3.0K), Hawaii Yogger ($2.6K), Lite 15" ($2.3K), Hybrid ($0.9K) and collab and artist versions sold about $12.2K. Their counterparts this year sold $1.2K.

Yogger pants and jackets grew ($8.9K vs $6.5K), so the whole Yogger franchise is down $7.4K, not $9.4K.

Sport Vent

−$5.8K

$9.7K vs $15.5K, units 179 vs 351. The Sport Vent SL Tank sold $3.6K last year; this year’s two sleeveless versions sold $0.9K. The core Sport Vent tee fell 16% ($6.7K vs $7.9K). Hawaii Sport Vent styles ($2.3K last year) have no sales this year.

That’ll Do shirts

−$5.2K

$7.8K vs $13.0K. Holds the largest single product miss: That’ll Do Stretch Short Sleeve Shirt sold $6.6K last October and has no sales this October. The Dobby, Seersucker and Stretch Long Sleeve versions sold $7.1K, but the Stripe Short Sleeve, Stretch Woven LS and That’ll Work Flannel ($4.2K together last year) left a gap.

Graphic and core tees

−$7.6K

Units down 35% (1,588 vs 2,424), dollars down 12%. Net per tee rose from $26 to $35. Last October’s top graphics sold at roughly $18–25 gross per unit (VA All The Way, Puerto Nuevo, Exotica), while this year’s new graphics sell at $34–40. The export does not show list price, but the pattern points to lower tee pricing last year.

Carried tees that lost volume: VA All The Way (21 units vs 124), VA ATW Hawaii (9 vs 80), Icon (9 vs 73).

Hoodies

−$4.2K

$11.4K vs $15.6K, units 178 vs 296. Big RVCA Hoodie fell from 108 units to 38 (−$2.5K), with far less discounting on it this year ($119 vs $708). VA Essential, Global Rank and Frame Up hoodies ($4.4K last year) have no sales this year. Crew sweatshirts held flat.

Hats

−$2.9K

$10.2K vs $13.1K. 53 hat styles sold vs 74 last year, while units per style held steady (6.5 vs 6.2). The miss tracks a narrower hat assortment, not weaker selling per style.

VA X Ali collab

−$3.6K

Nine styles sold $3.6K last October; none sold this October. Hoodie, sweatpant, Yogger short, tees and hats. The new Tapout collab added $1.5K.

Escape shorts

−$1.6K

$2.4K vs $4.0K. The Escape Elastic Short fell from 55 units to 10 (−$2.1K). The new Escape Elastic Cord Short added $1.2K.

Data notes
  • Source: Shopify “Total sales by product” export, labeled Oct 1–21, 2026, with Shopify’s previous-year columns as the comparison. The file covers the 21st, not Oct 1–20.
  • Metric: Net sales (gross minus discounts and returns, before tax). “Net $ per unit” is net sales divided by net units.
  • Excluded: Authentic Membership (677 units at $10 total) and Route shipping protection, so units reflect product only.
  • Total sales including tax was $272,863 vs $249,313 (+9.4%). It grows slower than net sales because tax collected fell from $16,928 to $8,339. This file cannot explain the tax drop; check it before quoting the dashboard’s Total sales figure.
  • Gender split is assigned from product type plus name keywords (women’s-only types such as dresses and bikinis, and women’s style names). Hats, bags, belts and wallets count as accessories.
  • Franchise lines are matched on product name, first match wins (a Hawaii Yogger short counts under Yogger).
  • Discounts in Shopify cover discount codes and automatic discounts. Sale pricing set through compare-at price does not show up as a discount, which is why tee pricing is read from gross dollars per unit.
