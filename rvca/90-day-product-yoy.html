The read

Sales grew 30%, all of it from new product. The styles carried over from last year are selling less.

  1. Net sales grew $301K on 15% more units and a 13% higher dollar value per unit. Discounting was lighter (8.8% of gross vs 11.6%).
  2. Products that sold in both periods are down 12% ($523.5K vs $595.5K). New products sold $784.9K, 60% of the period. Over 90 days the new lines are carrying the business while the established ones slip. The October-only view showed carried styles up 18%, so that trend may be turning, but it is three weeks of data.
  3. Sport Vent is the biggest miss by far at −$44.1K (−57%). The SL Tank ($22.5K last year) is gone, three Hawaii Sport Vent styles ($9.5K) are gone, and the core Sport Vent tee fell $15.0K.
  4. Hats are the biggest category miss at −$23.2K (−30%), on 25 fewer styles selling. Tanks (−$11.2K), hoodies (−$9.0K) and tees (−$8.3K) round out the misses.
  5. Unlike October, the core Yogger Stretch 17" is down here: −$13.7K (−13%) on 383 fewer units, with about half the discount dollars of last year.
  6. Returns rose 146% to $258,921, 15.1% of gross vs 8.4%. They absorbed a third of the gross sales increase. Swim accounts for $57.0K of the $153.7K increase.

03

The big misses, line by line

Franchise-level reads, so renamed and replacement styles are counted together. Dollar figures are net sales change vs the same 90 days last year.

Sport Vent

−$44.1K

$33.4K vs $77.5K, units 599 vs 1,646. The Sport Vent SL Tank sold $22.5K last year and has no sales this year; the two new sleeveless versions sold $1.2K. The core Sport Vent tee fell from $38.4K to $23.4K (420 units vs 761). Hawaii Sport Vent tee, tank and SL ($9.5K together) have no sales.

The new Sport Vent Long Sleeve Tee added $4.0K.

Hats

−$23.2K

$54.8K vs $78.0K, units 1,810 vs 2,711. 64 styles sold vs 89, and units per style dipped slightly (28 vs 30). Most of the gap is styles that sold last year and not this year (Frond Script, Islands Patch V, VA ATW Curved Brim, Blinders, Commonwealth). The RVCA Trucker fell from $4.3K to $1.9K. VA All The Way Trucker grew to $7.3K.

Yogger shorts

−$20.4K

$126.8K vs $147.2K, units 2,019 vs 2,647. Core Yogger Stretch 17" fell $13.7K ($89.9K vs $103.5K) with discounts on it at $8.0K vs $16.0K. Variants with little or no sales this year (2-in-1, Lite 15", Jesse Barba, VA X Ali, Essential 12", Hybrid, Hawaii Recover, Rich Jacobs, Plus 2) sold about $29.1K last year.

Yogger Stretch Contrast (+$13.2K) and Recover (+$5.7K) grew. Yogger pants and jackets grew to $24.8K from $15.7K, so the whole franchise is down $12.8K.

Tanks and Hawaii graphics

−$11.2K

Tank Tops type $20.4K vs $31.6K. Five tanks sold $23.8K last year and almost nothing this year: Hawaii Icon Band, Happy Hour, PTC Pigment, Puerto Nuevo and Global Rank. The Hawaii line overall is down $11.7K; the Hawaii Icon Band tank and tee ($16.7K) went away, and new Ben Horton Hawaii and Hawaii 4.0 tees ($10.3K) replaced part of it.

VA X Ali collab

−$11.7K

Ten styles sold $11.7K last year; none sold this year. Hoodie, sweatpant, tees, boxer short, Yogger short and hats. The new Tapout collab sold $15.0K, more than covering it.

Hoodies

−$9.0K

$28.5K vs $37.4K, units 426 vs 673. Big RVCA Hoodie fell from 200 units to 74 (−$5.3K). Global Rank, VA Essential, Frame Up, Puerto Nuevo and Surf Shirt hoodies sold $13.2K last year and almost nothing this year. Dayshift Hoodie grew to $3.7K.

Tees

−$8.3K

$293.1K vs $301.4K, units 8,837 vs 10,197. Dollars held better than units because each tee sold for more ($41 vs $36 gross per unit). Last year’s graphics that did not carry over include VA Mark Reflective ($9.9K), Puerto Nuevo ($7.3K), RVCA 2X Fill ($7.2K) and Blur Fill ($5.5K). New VAcancy Pocket Tee ($11.3K) and VA Mark 2 ($8.6K) led the replacements.

Escape shorts

−$4.8K

$18.5K vs $23.3K. Escape Elastic Short fell from 252 units to 29 (−$11.1K). The new Escape Elastic Cord Short added $9.0K.

Not real misses
Several of the largest product-level drops are replaced by a renamed style in the same line. Weekend Stretch Pant (−$20.6K) and Americana Chino 2 (−$16.2K) were replaced by Weekend 2, Weekend Washed and Americana 2 Relaxed chinos ($58.8K together); the Weekend line is up $34.2K and Americana up $17.9K. That’ll Do Stretch SS (−$16.3K) was offset by That’ll Do Seersucker ($13.9K), leaving the line down only $2.3K. Compact Short Sleeve Shirt (−$15.5K) has no direct successor, but shirts overall grew $47.7K.

Data notes
  • Source: Shopify “Total sales by product” export, labeled Jul 20 to Oct 20, 2026 (93 days inclusive), with Shopify’s previous-year columns as the comparison.
  • Metric: Net sales (gross minus discounts and returns, before tax). “Net $ per unit” is net sales divided by net units.
  • Excluded: Authentic Membership and Route shipping protection, so units reflect product only.
  • Total sales including tax was $1,347,879 vs $1,076,264 (+25.2%). It grows slower than net sales because tax collected fell from $69,600 to $40,547. This file cannot explain the tax drop; check it before quoting the dashboard’s Total sales figure.
  • Gender split is assigned from product type plus name keywords (women’s-only types such as dresses and bikinis, and women’s style names). Hats, bags, belts and wallets count as accessories.
  • Franchise lines are matched on product name, first match wins (a Hawaii Yogger short counts under Yogger).
  • Discounts in Shopify cover discount codes and automatic discounts. Sale pricing set through compare-at price does not show up as a discount, which is why tee pricing is read from gross dollars per unit.
