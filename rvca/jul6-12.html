Persona A/B Split Test

Jul 11 ran a six-persona A/B test on the New Arrivals send. Version A was persona-personalized (products and copy tuned to each persona); Version B was the control — general New Arrivals imagery, not persona-targeted. Each ran a clean 50/50 full send. Klaviyo declares its winner on click rate; the business metric is revenue. They diverge — both are shown.

Headline · personalization won attention, control edged the sale
4 / 6
Personas where the persona version won on click rate — Klaviyo's declared winner.
5 / 6
Personas where the control edged revenue — the general New Arrivals creative converted.
1 / 6
Clean sweep — Everyday Visionary won both clicks and revenue for the persona version.
The read, and it matches the brief going in: persona personalization reliably lifted engagement, but did not convert that attention into revenue on a New Arrivals offer — there wasn't enough persona-fit imagery for new product. The argument is to rerun on best-sellers, where the imagery library is deep enough to give personalization a fair shot at the sale.

⚠ Read note · two metrics, one test — don't read Klaviyo's flag as the revenue winner

Klaviyo's Winning Variant flag is decided on click rate, so it reads the persona version as the winner in four of six segments. On revenue, the control won five of six. Both are true — they measure different things. Reporting only the platform flag would overstate the personalization result.

Design context: the test swapped the email creative, not the subject line — Version A carried persona products and copy, Version B carried general New Arrivals imagery. New arrivals had limited persona-fit imagery, which is the likely reason personalization won attention but not the sale.

Next: rerun the split on best-sellers, where the imagery range is deep enough to personalize the offer itself — that is the test that shows whether persona personalization moves revenue, not just clicks.
